# Role Tailoring Reference

Use this guide to decide what to emphasise, how to word bullets, and what to include/exclude based on the user's target role and company type.

---

## By Role Type

### Software Engineering (SWE / Backend / Full-Stack / Frontend)
**Emphasise**: Systems built, scale handled, tech stack depth, performance wins, architecture decisions.
**Bullet language**: Active verbs — Built, Architected, Optimised, Reduced, Shipped, Deployed, Migrated, Refactored.
**Quantify**: Latency reductions, uptime %, lines of code, users served, requests/sec, load time improvements.
**Include**: Projects with full-stack depth, open-source contributions, system design decisions.
**De-emphasise**: Soft leadership unless applying to senior/staff roles.

### Machine Learning / AI / Data Science
**Emphasise**: Models trained, accuracy/F1/AUC metrics, datasets used, novel architectures, research contributions.
**Bullet language**: Trained, Developed, Achieved, Evaluated, Benchmarked, Implemented, Analysed.
**Quantify**: Accuracy %, latency, dataset size, compute savings, benchmark scores, paper citations.
**Include**: Research papers, Kaggle/competition rankings, frameworks (PyTorch, TF, JAX), SHAP/explainability work.
**De-emphasise**: Web dev work unless it directly served an ML product.

### Research (Academic / Lab / R&D)
**Emphasise**: Novel contributions, formal rigour, publications/preprints, mathematical depth, collaboration with domain experts.
**Bullet language**: Developed, Formalised, Proved, Derived, Investigated, Proposed, Collaborated.
**Quantify**: # theorems, # papers, citation counts, olympiad ranks, competition placements.
**Include**: Research papers, olympiad achievements, academic awards, relevant coursework, GPA prominently.
**De-emphasise**: Commercial projects unless they demonstrate research application.

### Product Management
**Emphasise**: Problem definition, user impact, cross-functional collaboration, shipped features, data-driven decisions.
**Bullet language**: Defined, Led, Shipped, Increased, Reduced, Partnered, Launched, Prioritised.
**Quantify**: Revenue impact, user growth %, retention, NPS, feature adoption, time-to-ship improvements.
**Include**: Projects where you were the driver/decision-maker, any user research or A/B testing.
**De-emphasise**: Deep technical implementation details; keep tech mentions high-level.

### Quant / Finance (Trading, Hedge Fund, IB)
**Emphasise**: Mathematical rigour, statistical modelling, performance metrics (Sharpe, alpha), speed/latency, data pipelines.
**Bullet language**: Modelled, Backtested, Optimised, Engineered, Achieved, Computed, Simulated.
**Quantify**: Returns %, Sharpe ratio, latency in µs/ms, dataset size, model accuracy on financial benchmarks.
**Include**: Olympiad achievements, competitive maths, statistics-heavy projects, any trading/econ coursework.
**De-emphasise**: Web apps or soft-skill-heavy roles unless quantitatively framed.

### Consulting / Strategy
**Emphasise**: Structured problem-solving, stakeholder communication, research synthesis, recommendations made, business impact.
**Bullet language**: Analysed, Recommended, Presented, Synthesised, Designed, Advised, Delivered.
**Quantify**: Cost savings, efficiency gains %, number of stakeholders, project scope, timeline.
**Include**: Any strategy/roadmap work, multi-stakeholder projects, written deliverables.
**De-emphasise**: Highly technical implementation details; reframe them as business impact.

### Startups / Early-Stage
**Emphasise**: Breadth, ownership, speed of execution, shipped products, real users/traction, initiative.
**Bullet language**: Built, Launched, Owned, Shipped, Grew, Managed, Designed, Drove.
**Quantify**: Users, downloads, revenue, uptime, performance wins, time from idea to launch.
**Include**: Side projects, indie products, any entrepreneurial work, full-stack ownership.
**De-emphasise**: Academic formality; reframe research as applied impact.

## By Company Type

### FAANG / Big Tech (Google, Meta, Amazon, Apple, Microsoft, etc.)
- Strong system design and scalability signals preferred.
- Metrics and impact at scale are critical.
- Academic pedigree (GPA, university ranking, competitions) matters more here.
- Bullet depth: 2–3 lines, heavily quantified.

### Growth-Stage Startups (Series A–C)
- Full-stack ownership and shipping velocity matter most.
- Show you can wear multiple hats.
- Traction metrics (users, revenue) highly valued over academic signals.
- Bullet depth: 1–2 punchy lines each.

### Early-Stage / Seed Startups
- Emphasise raw initiative, breadth, and speed.
- Personal projects and side products are gold.
- Culture fit signals: interests, personality, passion projects.

### Consulting Firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, etc.)
- Structured, businesss-impact framing for every bullet.
- Communication and leadership signals are key.
- GPA and prestige signals (olympiads, scholarships) matter greatly.

### Hedge Funds / Trading Firms (Jane Street, Citadel, Two Sigma, etc.)
- Mathematical competition history is a huge signal.
- Quant modelling and statistics skills front and centre.
- Clean, precise, no fluff. Every word earns its place.

### Research Labs (DeepMind, OpenAI, MIRI, academic labs)
- Research papers, novel contributions, and mathematical depth dominate.
- Academic awards, olympiad results, and GPA prominently featured.
- De-emphasise web dev; reframe any ML work as research contribution.

| Role | Recommended Order |
|------|------------------|
| SWE / Full-Stack | Education → Experience → Projects → Skills |
| ML / AI | Education → Experience → Projects → Skills |
| Research | Education → Publications/Research → Experience → Skills |
| PM | Education → Experience → Projects → Skills |
| Quant / Finance | Education → Experience → Projects → Skills |
| Consulting | Education → Experience → Projects → Skills |
| Startup (lots of projects) | Education → Projects → Experience → Skills |

For new graduates / students with limited experience, put the most impressive section second (after Education) — often Projects or a strong Research entry.

## Bullet Writing: The CAR Framework

Every bullet in the Experience and Projects sections must follow the **CAR format**:
**Challenge → Action → Result**

This is the single most important principle in this skill. Do not write a bullet that is missing any of the three components.

### What each component means

| Component | Definition | Key question |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| **Challenge** | The situation, problem, constraint, or opportunity that made the work non-trivial | *Why did this need doing? What was broken, missing, or at stake?* |
| **Action** | The specific thing YOU did to address it — verb-led, precise, technical where relevant | *What exactly did you do? What decisions did you make?* |
| **Result** | The measurable outcome — **must be a number wherever humanly possible** | *What changed? By how much? How fast? How many?* |

### Quantifying the Result — non-negotiable

A Result without a number is a claim. A Result with a number is evidence. Hiring managers read dozens of bullets saying "improved performance" and "reduced costs" — the ones that stop them are "reduced p99 latency from 800ms to 120ms" and "cut infrastructure costs by 40%".

**Always try to attach a number to the Result.** If the user hasn't provided one, ask before writing the bullet. Good prompts to extract numbers:

- *"By how much did it improve? Even a rough estimate is fine."*
- *"How many users / requests / records / people did this affect?"*
- *"How long did it take before vs. after?"*
- *"What was the accuracy / error rate / uptime?"*
- *"How much time or money did it save — per week, per month, per run?"*

**Types of numbers that work well:**

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| Performance | Latency ↓ 70%, throughput ↑ 3×, uptime 99.9% |
| Scale | 10K users, 50M rows, 200 requests/sec |
| Accuracy | 93% validation accuracy, F1 0.91, ±0.11% SE |
| Speed | 2× faster, cut build time from 8min to 90sec |
| Cost / efficiency | 3–5× cost reduction, saved 12 hrs/week |
| Scope | 15+ architectures, 100K simulations, 5 core theorems |
| Impact | 2K+ downloads, 4.5/5 avg rating, 30% engagement lift |

**When there genuinely is no number:** write the bullet without one — but flag it inline so the user can fill it in later:

```
% [METRIC MISSING — ask user: e.g. how many users, what accuracy, how much faster?]
```

Never invent a number. A flagged placeholder is far better than a fabricated metric.

### The golden rule: one bullet, all three

CAR does **not** mean three separate bullets. Compress all three components into a single flowing sentence of 1–2 lines. The Challenge is often just a clause or a few words that gives context; the Action is the core; the Result is the payoff.

**Structure:**
```
[Action verb] [what you built/did] [to address Challenge context], [achieving/resulting in quantified Result]
```

### Before / After examples

**❌ Activity-only (no CAR):**
> Worked on AI architecture comparison for the team.

**✅ Full CAR:**
> Compared 15+ AI architectures (LLMs, SLMs, SLM+RAG) across latency, cost, and reliability to guide Mahindra's deployment strategy, recommending an SLM-first design that cut projected inference latency by 70%.

---

**❌ Action + Result but no Challenge:**
> Built CataractCNN achieving 93% validation accuracy.

**✅ Full CAR:**
> Developed CataractCNN, a lightweight CNN for offline cataract detection in rural India where specialist access is limited, achieving 93% validation accuracy with sub-1-second on-device inference on mid-range Android devices.

---

**❌ Challenge + Action but no Result:**
> Faced with poor inventory tracking, redesigned the stock management system using a new database schema.

**✅ Full CAR:**
> Redesigned inventory tracking system to eliminate manual stock discrepancies, reducing order fulfilment time by 20% and cutting supplier delays by 20% over six months.

---

### When the Challenge is implicit

For technical projects, the challenge is sometimes obvious from context (e.g., "built a cataract detection app" — the challenge is clear). In these cases, embed the challenge as a brief clause rather than a full sentence:

> Built an offline cataract screening app for rural India **[Challenge: no specialist access]**, achieving 93% accuracy with sub-1s inference on mid-range devices **[Result]**.

Compressed:
> Built an offline mobile cataract screening app to extend specialist-level diagnosis to rural India, achieving 93% validation accuracy and sub-1-second inference on mid-range Android devices.

The challenge ("extend specialist-level diagnosis to rural India") is woven in naturally.

### CAR by role type

Different roles weight the three components differently:

| Role | Emphasise most | Typical Result metric |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| SWE | Action (what you built) | Latency ↓, uptime %, users served |
| ML / AI | Action + Result | Accuracy %, F1, compute savings |
| Quant | Challenge (rigour of problem) + Result | Sharpe, α, simulation SE, latency µs |
| PM | Challenge (user problem) + Result | Retention %, revenue, adoption |
| Consulting | Challenge (business problem) + Result | Cost savings, efficiency %, stakeholders |
| Research | Challenge (novelty) + Action (method) | # theorems, benchmarks, citations |
| Startup | Action (ownership) + Result | Users, revenue, time-to-ship |

### Asking users for CAR components

When the user's raw notes are missing the Challenge or Result, ask targeted questions before drafting:

- **Missing Challenge**: "What was the problem or constraint that made this work necessary?"
- **Missing Result**: "What changed after you did this? Any numbers — even rough estimates — like time saved, accuracy improvement, users, or cost reduction?"
- **Missing Action specificity**: "What did you specifically build or decide? What was your individual contribution vs. the team's?"

Never fabricate a number. Write the bullet without the metric and flag it:
> `% [ADD METRIC — ask user for accuracy/latency/users]`

### Additional bullet principles

1. **Lead the Action with a strong verb** — never start with "I", a noun, or a passive construction.
2. **Quantify the Result with a number** — if you can't find an exact figure, use a qualifier ("up to 70%", "over 10K", "approximately 3×"). A rough number beats no number.
3. **One idea per bullet** — if a bullet contains "and" linking two separate achievements, split into two bullets first. Cut the weaker one only if splitting still exceeds 2 lines or one idea is clearly weaker than the other.
4. **Tailor vocabulary to the reader** — SWE: "microservices", "distributed systems"; Quant: "Monte Carlo", "Sharpe ratio"; Consulting: "stakeholder alignment", "strategic roadmap".
5. **Prefer specificity over vagueness** — "reduced p99 latency from 800ms to 120ms" beats "significantly improved performance".

## ⚠️ The 2-Line Hard Limit

Every bullet must fit within 2 printed lines at 10pt font in the Jake Ryan template. This is a hard constraint, not a guideline.

### Why this matters

At 10pt in the Jake Ryan template, the text width is approximately 90 characters. A bullet that runs to 3 lines wastes vertical space, reduces scannability, and signals poor editing discipline to a hiring manager. Resume bullets are headlines, not sentences.

### How to enforce it

Before writing any bullet, mentally apply this test:

> Can I say this in one tightly packed sentence that fits within 90 characters of usable text per line, across at most 2 lines?

If not, cut. The question is always: what is the single most impactful thing this bullet needs to communicate? Cut the rest.

### Cutting protocol — in order

1. **Split before cutting** — if the bullet contains "and" linking two separate achievements, first try splitting into two bullets. Cut the weaker one only if splitting still leaves a bullet over 2 lines, or if one idea is clearly the weaker of the two.
2. **Compress the Challenge** — the challenge often needs just a clause ("to reduce rural healthcare gaps"), not a full sentence.
3. **Abbreviate the tech stack** — in a bullet, list only the 2–3 most distinctive tools; the full stack is on the project heading line.
4. **Cut qualifiers** — "in a highly scalable, production-ready manner" → delete entirely.
5. **Merge redundant clauses** — "built X, then deployed X, then monitored X" → "built and deployed X, monitoring Y."

### Before / After

**❌ Too long (3 lines):**
> Built a web app for startup founders to surface AI subscription waste; deterministic rules engine runs against live vendor pricing to flag downgrades, redundant tools, and better-fit alternatives; each audit generates a shareable URL with an AI-written CFO-tone summary in under 3 minutes

**✅ 2 lines max:**
> Built a tool that audits startup AI subscriptions against live vendor pricing, surfacing prioritised downgrade paths and redundant tools; each audit generates a shareable URL with a CFO-tone summary in under 3 minutes

## Content Pruning by Space

If the resume exceeds 1 page at 10pt font, prune in this order:
1. Trim weakest bullets (least quantified, least relevant to role).
2. Reduce project count — keep the 3–5 most relevant.
3. Merge short experience entries (e.g., 1-bullet roles) with a note.
4. Shorten education bullets if GPA/awards are on the subheading line.
5. Collapse skills into fewer, denser lines.
